A second brain that files itself

How to turn your Obsidian vault into a brain your AI builds and keeps current for you, every night, without being asked: what it is, how to set it up, how to use it, and the rules your AI follows.

What this is

What's a wiki? A collection of pages that link to each other, like Wikipedia. Each page covers one thing: a person, an idea, an article you read. When one page mentions something that has its own page, it links there, so you can click from an idea to the author who wrote about it to the study they cited. Yours is private, lives in Obsidian as plain files on your Mac, and only contains what came from your own reading and work.

What's an LLM wiki? The idea comes from Andrej Karpathy's LLM Wiki. Most AI tools re-read your documents from scratch every time you ask something, so nothing builds up. Here it works the other way round. Your AI reads each source once, files what matters into linked pages, and keeps those pages current as new material arrives. Ask something next month and the answer is already organised, with the contradictions flagged and the links in place.

Why "files itself". A wiki that waits for you to say "file this" doesn't grow. Nobody remembers to. So every night your AI files what mattered from yesterday's conversations and your new clippings. You just clip things and keep working. Every change is logged, every fact has a source, and anything can be undone.

How it differs from Karpathy's original

The core follows his pattern closely: raw sources that are never edited, pages the AI owns, an index, a log, and a rules file. The differences:

Setting it up changes where some files live, so it starts with a full backup. Nothing gets deleted, and your AI shows you the plan and waits for your go before it moves a single file.

Part 1: Set it up

Before you start: a WATNEY kit install (your AI running in Claude Code, with a vault in Obsidian and the nightly memory job). It also works on another Obsidian vault; your AI will ask where it is. Allow about 20 minutes.

  1. Quit Obsidian. It can trip over files being moved while it's open.
  2. Start a Claude Code session with your AI and paste in the prompt below.
  3. Answer one question: do you keep a daily journal in Obsidian? If yes, it gets its own folder that only you write in. If no, daily notes are switched off.
  4. Read the plan your AI shows you, then say "go". If something in the plan looks wrong, say so first.
  5. Say yes or no to the skill fixes. Some of your AI's skills may point at old folder names. It asks about each one.
  6. Change a few things in Obsidian once it's done:
    • Journal: Settings → Daily notes → New file location: Daily logs. No journal: Settings → Core plugins → switch off Daily notes.
    • Install the Obsidian Web Clipper browser extension and set its save folder to Clippings
    • Settings → Files and links → Attachment folder path: Clippings/assets
    • Settings → Hotkeys → search "Download attachments for current file" and give it a shortcut. After clipping an article, press it so the images are saved where your AI can see them.
  7. Watch it file once. Your AI adds the filing step to its nightly job, then files up to 3 things from your setup conversation by hand, so you can see what a filed page looks like and how "undo that" works.
  8. Switch on search by meaning. Your AI walks you through installing the free Smart Connections plugin (the first toggle, "Turn on community plugins", is the one people miss). It asks before a one-time download of about 700 MB. After that, searching "pricing" also finds the page that says "what to charge". Everything stays on your Mac.
  9. Tell it what the brain is for. Your AI asks six short questions: what you want help with, what you'll clip, what you hope to ask it later, and what it should never file. Answer in your own words. That's what makes it your brain instead of a generic one.
  10. Check tomorrow morning. Ask "what did you file last night?"

Part 2: How to use it, day to day

Mostly: nothing. Clip what you care about and keep working. Your AI files overnight. When you want something, plain language works.

Save something you read
Just clip itNew clippings are filed overnight. Want it now, with a chat about what matters? Say "file this now". Or paste a link and say "file this".
Keep a decision
Just say it in a chat"We're going with the annual plan." The nightly run picks up decisions, new facts about people and projects, and insights worth keeping, and files them on the right page with the date.
See what got filed
"What did you file last night?"A short summary from the log. Your AI also mentions it in one line at the start of your first chat of the day.
Undo it
"Undo last night's filing"Every page is copied before it changes, so your AI can put last night's pages back exactly as they were. Or point at one page: "that's wrong, put it back".
Keep something out
"Don't file anything about my health"Added to the brain's "never file" list after your AI confirms.
Ask a question
"What do my sources say about pricing a workshop?"Your AI answers from the brain and links the pages it used. If it doesn't know enough, it says so and suggests what to read.
Keep a good answer
"Save that"The answer becomes its own page in _Brain/explorations/, so it isn't lost in the chat.
Find anything
"Have I read anything about sleep?"Your AI searches by meaning, so it finds the right pages even when they use different words than you do. In Obsidian, the Smart Connections panel does the same for whatever note you have open.
See what you've learned
"What have we learned so far?"Your AI keeps one page, _Brain/overview.md, that sums up what the brain knows, topic by topic, and how sure it is. A running list at the bottom shows each time new material changed the picture.
Keep it healthy
"Check my brain"Every few weeks. Finds contradictions, out-of-date claims, duplicate pages and loose ends, and suggests what to read next.
Think out loud
"What patterns do you see in my notes?"For this it reads only your own writing, never its own pages, so what comes back is really yours.
Rethink the focus
"Let's rethink what the brain is for"Runs the setup questions again. Worth doing every few months, or when your interests shift.
Change how it works
"From now on, do X"It suggests the exact change to its rules and waits for your yes.

Where to look in Obsidian

  • _Brain/overview.md is the one page to read: what the brain currently believes, and how that has changed.
  • _Brain/index.md is the catalogue: every page, one line each.
  • _Brain/log.md shows what was filed and when, including every nightly run.
  • Graph view shows how pages connect. A dot with no lines is a page nothing links to yet.

What your AI won't do

  • Change or delete anything you clipped.
  • Write in Notes/, _context/ or your journal. If you want something changed there, it writes the text in chat for you to paste.
  • File anything on your "never file" list, or more than 15 pages in one night.
  • Delete anything. Files that should leave the active vault move to Archive/, unchanged and still findable, and only when you ask, when two pages get merged into one, or when you undo a page it created. It never archives something just because it looks old; it asks first.
